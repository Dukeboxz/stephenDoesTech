Applying for jobs is a bit like therapy. It makes you revisit your career, and lately I've been thinking about the three questions junior colleagues always asked me when a restructure landed.

I've been through a lot of corporate change, some good and some bad, and I've often been the person helping someone navigate it for the first time. Here's what they ask, and what I tell them.

## 1. "Why wasn't XYZ thought about when this decision was made?"

The decision's made. The restructure's happening. Then someone notices we don't have the systems to support it, or that a person being moved is the one keeping something critical running.

Big decisions are usually made a few levels up, where the view is wide but the detail is thin. They're then handed to middle managers to make work, who get squeezed from both sides: pressure from above to deliver, and pushback from below because it can't be delivered as planned.

My advice: be the person who brings solutions, not just problems. That person is always valuable when change happens.

## 2. "Why weren't the people who know how it works included?"

This one usually follows straight after.

In my experience, the decision is often made well before the meeting that makes it official. Conversations have happened. People know which way the wind's blowing. By the formal meeting, there's little room left to shape it.

So my advice is to learn a bit of judo: don't resist the momentum, redirect it.

Decisions rarely come out of nowhere. Beforehand, you'll get pointed questions. How does this work? What would happen if we did that? Pay attention to those hypotheticals. They're your chance to show your manager where an idea leads, where the pain points are, and a better way forward, before it becomes something you're asked to implement.

## 3. "Why are they changing things when we're still reeling from the last change?"

By now the frustration is usually obvious.

Change is a constant. Businesses merge, reorganise, bring in new leaders. It will keep happening throughout your career.

My advice: work out what matters to you in your job, focus on what you can influence, and spend your energy there. The things you can't control will still happen, whether you worry about them or not.

As the old saying goes: "Grant me the serenity to accept the things I cannot change, the courage to change the things I can, and the wisdom to know the difference."

That's what you need to do. Pick your battles, fight the ones you can win, and stay true to yourself.
